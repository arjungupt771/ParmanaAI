WHO Fever Guidelines — Short Summary

According to the World Health Organization (WHO):
Fever is a symptom, not a disease — always focus on finding the underlying cause.
Assess severity first: look for danger signs (breathing difficulty, confusion, dehydration, persistent vomiting, seizures).
Do not treat fever aggressively just to reduce temperature — fever can help the immune response.
Antipyretics (paracetamol/ibuprofen) should be used only for comfort, not routinely.
Hydration and rest are essential parts of fever care.
Avoid unnecessary physical cooling (like sponging) unless clinically required.
High-risk groups (infants, elderly, pregnant people, chronic illness patients) need closer monitoring.
Refer urgently if fever is persistent, very high, or accompanied by red-flag symptoms.
