Diabetes mellitus is a chronic metabolic disorder characterized by persistent hyperglycaemia resulting from defects in insulin secretion, insulin action, or both, leading to disturbances in carbohydrate, fat, and protein metabolism.

Diagnosis of diabetes is based on elevated blood glucose levels, confirmed by standardized laboratory testing, and should not rely on symptoms alone unless hyperglycaemia is severe.

Types of diabetes include Type 1 diabetes (absolute insulin deficiency), Type 2 diabetes (insulin resistance with relative insulin deficiency), gestational diabetes, and other specific types caused by genetic defects, diseases of the pancreas, or drug-induced mechanisms.

Management of diabetes emphasizes early diagnosis, lifestyle modification, and long-term glycaemic control to prevent complications. Healthy diet, regular physical activity, and weight management are the foundation of treatment for all types.

Pharmacological therapy is added when lifestyle measures are insufficient. Metformin is recommended as first-line therapy for most adults with Type 2 diabetes, while insulin therapy is essential for Type 1 diabetes and advanced or uncontrolled cases.

Glycaemic control is monitored using HbA1c, fasting plasma glucose, and self-monitoring where appropriate, with individualized targets based on age, comorbidities, and risk of hypoglycaemia.

Prevention of complications is a central goal and includes control of blood glucose, blood pressure, and lipids, along with routine screening for cardiovascular disease, kidney disease, neuropathy, and retinopathy.

Public health focus in WHO guidelines stresses prevention, early screening, equitable access to essential medicines (especially insulin), and patient education, particularly in low- and middle-income countries.

📌 WHO core principle:
👉 Diabetes is lifelong, preventable in many cases, manageable in all cases, and complications are largely avoidable with early and sustained care.
