Fever is defined as a regulated rise in core body temperature above the normal daily variation, usually ≥ 38°C (100.4°F), and is considered a clinical sign of underlying infection or inflammatory process, not a disease by itself.

Evaluation of fever should be systematic and context-specific, with early identification of life-threatening conditions such as sepsis, malaria, dengue, typhoid, pneumonia, tuberculosis, and COVID-19, which are epidemiologically relevant in India.

Initial assessment must focus on duration of fever, associated symptoms, localizing signs, travel history, seasonal outbreaks, and comorbidities, along with identification of danger signs (altered sensorium, breathlessness, hypotension, bleeding, seizures).

Management of fever prioritizes supportive care, including adequate hydration, rest, and antipyretics (paracetamol preferred) for symptom relief. Fever reduction is aimed at comfort, not normalization of body temperature.

Antibiotics should not be used empirically for all fevers and must be prescribed only when a bacterial cause is clinically suspected or confirmed, in line with antimicrobial stewardship principles promoted by ICMR.

Investigations are guided by clinical presentation and duration:

Acute undifferentiated fever → rule out malaria, dengue, COVID-19

Fever >7 days → evaluate for typhoid, TB, bacterial infections, autoimmune causes

Special populations (infants, elderly, pregnant women, immunocompromised patients) require earlier referral and lower thresholds for investigation and hospitalization.

📌 ICMR core principle:
👉 Fever should be managed by identifying and treating the cause, avoiding irrational antibiotic use, and aligning care with India-specific disease patterns.
